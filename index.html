FIELD LAB REPORT // PROJECT 01

Real-Time Classroom Energy Monitoring System

STATUS: IN ACTIVE PROTOTYPE DEVELOPMENT
PLATE 5.1 — Laboratory breadboard & sensor rig showing Arduino UNO, ZMPT101B voltage sensor, ACS712 current sensor, terminal block, and electrical load bulb test fixture.

Problem Statement

Classroom energy consumption is traditionally recorded only on monthly bulk utility meters, making it difficult to continuously monitor discrete loads, pinpoint peak demand periods, eliminate idle off-hour waste, and detect abnormal electrical draw.

Engineered Solution

The system aims to capture electrical load information and transform it into understandable real-time and historical energy data via microcontroller telemetry and a digital monitoring interface.

Key Technical Features

  • Real-time classroom current (A) and voltage (V) monitoring
  • Continuous active power (W) and cumulative energy consumption tracking (kWh)
  • Estimated electricity cost calculation based on tariff algorithms
  • Live IoT digital telemetry dashboard and historical load curve profiling
  • Peak-load identification and threshold over-current/abnormal consumption alerts
  • Modular instrumentation scalable across multi-classroom campus networks
FIELD LAB REPORT // PROJECT 02

Smart Cooking Oil Impurity Detection System

STATUS: PRELIMINARY BENCH STUDY & RESEARCH
PLATE 6.1 — Experimental measurement bench featuring the HIOKI Impedance Analyzer, dedicated PC interface software, multi-well test fixture with sensor array, and oil sample container.

System Concept

A low-cost sensor-based concept for preliminary screening of changes in cooking-oil condition by analysing measurable physical and electrochemical parameters.

Measurement Parameters

Water/moisture contamination, pH variation, temperature characteristics, and impedance properties.

Key Capabilities & Advanced Exploration

  • Sensor-based comparative quality analysis between fresh and repeated-use oil
  • Real-time IoT dashboard data logging and threshold-based abnormal warnings
  • Potential parameters: Turbidity/optical sensing, color analysis, and electrical conductivity comparison
  • Exploration of Total Polar Compounds (TPC) and ML-based classification models
RESEARCH DISCLAIMER: This system is intended strictly as a preliminary research and screening concept. Sensor readings require calibration and validation against established laboratory methods before being used for actual food-safety decisions.
